A live art game for one room

Three people.Three dials.One artwork.

Phones scan a QR. Three Creators each own one dial of the same piece on the big screen: palette, form, or motion. Everyone else is a Spectator and rates what the room made. Nobody can finish it alone.

Host a sessionOn the laptop. Makes the code, the QR and the big screen.
Play the 60-second demoNo phone, no room. Just you, three dials and the three judges.

Murmuration, n. — a flock of starlings turning as one shape, with no leaderMurmuration

01

One dial each. Nobody holds the whole thing.

The first three phones to join become Creators. Each one owns a single dial of the shared scene and picks from four options written for that scene. One tap changes hundreds of things on screen; the other two dials belong to someone else.

    02

    The screen eases toward whatever the room decides.

    A prompt is drawn from the scene (“Calm but urgent”, “It is six PM and you are already late”). The timer runs on server time. Every change glides in over about a second; nothing jump-cuts. At zero the piece locks, settles, and is captured. That frame is what gets judged.

    Live60, 90 or 120 s. Creators can change their mind until zero.
    LockNo take-backs.
    Settle1.5 s hush.
    Rate12–25 s. Spectators tap 1–5, once.
    RevealThree scores, one verdict.
    03

    Three judges. One of them is the room.

    Scores show separately, then combine. The artwork is judged, never the people.

    Scene Author’s taste

    Whoever made the scene ranked every option before the round. Deterministic, hidden until the reveal.

    No AI
    AI judge

    Sees the locked frame once, after the round, and speaks a 20-second verdict out loud. It never touches the live canvas.

    After lock only
    Audience

    Everyone who is not holding a dial rates 1–5 from their phone. Trimmed mean, one vote per device.
